# RESEARCH.md (template)

Copy this file to `RESEARCH.md` and fill it in. Target roughly **300–500
words**, excluding the reference list. Prose is fine; bullets are fine. Delete
the guidance text as you go.

The purpose of this document is to show *what you learned from sources* and
*where your own judgment took over*. Keep those two things visibly separate
throughout.

---

## 1. What DTT is

Define Discrete Trial Training in your own words. Do not paste definitions.
Say where it comes from, what problem it addresses, and what makes a trial
"discrete".

## 2. Sources used

At least **three** credible sources, at least **two** of which are
peer-reviewed, university, government, or recognised professional sources.

For each one, note in a sentence what you took from it. A source you cite but
did not actually use is worse than one fewer citation.

## 3. The elements of a trial

Which components do your sources say make up a trial? Where sources name the
same component differently, say so.

## 4. Responses and how they are handled

Discuss how the sources treat:

- a correct response
- an incorrect response
- an absent response

Note where sources agree, and where they leave the handling open.

## 5. Procedural topics

Cover each of these **to the extent your sources support it**. If a source is
silent on one, say that rather than filling the gap with an unsourced claim —
you will get your chance to fill it in `PROTOCOL.md`, labelled as your own
decision.

- prompting
- prompt fading, or transfer of stimulus control
- reinforcement
- error correction
- movement between trials
- what is recorded per trial
- when a session ends

## 6. Where sources disagree

Identify at least one real point of variation or disagreement you found across
your sources — in procedure, terminology, or emphasis. Explain what the
difference is and why it matters to someone trying to write this down as code.

A submission that reports "all my sources agreed on everything" usually means
the reading was too narrow.

## 8. Limitations

What is lost when a human-delivered intervention is compressed into a toy
software agent driven by three answer labels? Be concrete and be honest. This
section is graded on judgment, not on modesty.

Include a clear statement that this exercise is a simulation and not clinical
guidance.
